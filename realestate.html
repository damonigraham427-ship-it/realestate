The Broker Step — Full

What a broker is

A sales agent works under a broker's license and must be sponsored. A broker IS the license holder.

A broker can: sponsor himself (no sponsor needed), open his own brokerage, sponsor other sales agents, and keep the full commission minus his own business costs.

This is the only step in Texas real estate that lets you work for yourself legally.

Eligibility — all of it, TREC rule 22 TAC 535.56

Experience (no degree touches this part)

At least 4 years of ACTIVE experience as a licensed sales agent during the 5 years right before you apply. Inactive years do not count.

The experience is scored in points on the Qualifying Experience Report for a Broker License, with a transaction list attached. Sales, listings, and leases earn points.

Points total needed: 3,600 per TREC's published guidance and every provider source checked — BUT the Governor's Regulatory Compliance review letter (Oct 31, 2025) describing the same rule prints "a minimum of 360 experience points." UNRESOLVED conflict between official sources; TREC was amending this rule in late 2025. Fresh check required before relying on either number.

Education

270 hours of qualifying real estate courses total: the 180 from the sales agent license + 90 more, including a 30-hour Real Estate Brokerage course. The Brokerage course must be completed no more than 2 years before the application date.

PLUS 630 hours of real estate-related education — satisfied outright by a bachelor's degree or higher (rule 535.56(a)(2)). Damoni's BBA covers this block in full; the later degrees are surplus here.

New from January 1, 2026: a 6-hour broker responsibility course is also required (Occupations Code 1101.356(a)(3)).

Standing requirements

Meet TREC's honesty, trustworthiness, and integrity standard; fingerprints and background check as with the first license.

The broker exam

A separate, broker-level exam through Pearson VUE, taken only after TREC accepts the experience and education.

Fee: $58 per attempt per Pearson VUE's September 2026 candidate handbook (TREC's older fee schedule prints $39 — the handbook figure is what Pearson collects).

Damoni's position on this ladder

Education: effectively DONE years early — the BBA satisfies the 630-hour block; only the 90 extra qualifying hours (incl. Brokerage) would remain.

Experience: the whole wait. His sales license cannot go active for paid work until the Army gate clears (command approval, SkillBridge Jun 5, 2027, terminal leave, or post-ETS Dec 2, 2027).

Earliest realistic broker date (modeled): activation late 2027 + 4 active years = late 2031 at the very earliest, and only if the 4 years run without an inactive gap.

The PhD pathway neither helps nor hurts this ladder. They are parallel tracks: TREC counts years and deals; the universities count degrees.

SPONSOR DEALS THAT FIT YOUR SITUATION

Checked: 2026-11-19, from brokerages' published plans and 2026 fee comparisons

Rule: every figure below must be re-confirmed in writing with the brokerage before signing — plans change yearly

FIT LOGIC

• At low volume, fixed monthly fees hurt more than splits. A 30% split on 4 deals is cheaper than $85-$119/month forever

• Caps ($12,000-$16,000) only matter above ~$80,000 in commissions — a part-timer rarely caps, so ignore cap marketing

• What matters for you: $0 or low monthly, low per-deal fee, part-time allowed, real supervision

1. NB ELITE REALTY (Texas-based, Houston office) — BEST LOW-VOLUME FIT

• 100% commission from deal #1

• Annual brokerage fee: $350 upfront or $39/month

• Transaction fee: starts at $299, sliding scale by price, E&O included

• Investor-focused training (fits the development interest), sponsors across Texas

• Catch: built for self-sufficient agents — supervision is thinner than a training brokerage

2. FATHOM REALTY (cloud, Houston agents) — LOW FIXED COST, WATCH THE COMPANY

• Plans changed April 2026: Edge = 7% of commission to a $9,000 cap, then $165/deal; Elevate = 20% flat, no cap

• $75/month, $350 minimum per deal, $35 E&O per sale

• FLAG: Fathom's parent disclosed going-concern doubt in 2026 filings and agreed to be acquired — a sponsor's stability matters; ask what happens to your license and listings if the deal closes

3. EXP REALTY (cloud) — BEST MENTORSHIP OF THE CLOUD MODELS

• 80/20 split to a $16,000 cap, then 100%

• $149 startup, $85/month, $25 broker review + ~$40-$60 risk fee per deal

• New agents: mentor program takes an extra cut on your first 3 transactions (effectively 60/40) — that buys you a named mentor, which the flat-fee shops do not give

• Cost at 4 deals/yr: the $85/month = $1,020/yr runs even in academy months

4. REAL BROKERAGE (cloud) — MIDDLE GROUND

• 85/15 split to a $12,000 cap, then 100% ($285 per post-cap deal)

• No monthly fee. $900 annual fee taken from your first closings (if you don't close, you don't pay it), $50 per-deal CBR fee, $249 startup

• Houston teams exist on this platform (e.g. Houston Properties Team) — team membership changes the cap to $6,000 but adds a team split; ask both ways

5. KELLER WILLIAMS (Houston market centers) — BEST TRAINING, WORST PART-TIME MATH

• ~70/30 split to ~$18,000 company cap + $3,000 royalty cap, then 100%

• Monthly fees ~$50-$119 depending on market center, vary by office — Houston has many market centers and each sets its own fees

• Strongest new-agent training and local offices; the right pick only if you will actually attend training and produce enough to justify the split

6. LOCAL INDEPENDENT / FLAT-FEE SPONSOR — THE WILDCARD

• Houston has sponsor-only and small independent brokers: 100% commission, flat per-deal fee, no monthly — full fee schedules often not published (e.g. Texas Broker Sponsor)

• Cheapest possible holding pattern for a license, but supervision and support are the thinnest. Better as a later move than a first sponsor

VERDICT FOR YOUR SITUATION

• First sponsor, learning year: eXp (mentor) or a KW market center (training) if you accept the cost of learning

• First sponsor, cost-first: NB Elite — $350/yr + ~$299/deal is the cheapest credible structure found for 2-6 deals a year

• Decide with Step 28's question: total paid to the broker across 4 deals, in writing

Classes and books

Are the classes online?

Yes — all 180 hours can be taken 100% online, self-paced, from a TREC-approved provider (examples: Kaplan, Champions School of Real Estate, MLS Campus, The CE Shop)

TREC caps credit at 12 hours per day, so the six courses take at least ~18 days; typical finish is about 7 weeks

Each course ends with an online final; course access usually runs 6 months from enrollment

Packages run roughly $289–$668 depending on provider and tier

Order matters: get a REALM Portal account first and give your pre-license education number to the provider; finish the courses BEFORE filing the license application — the application expires 1 year after filing

Do they have a book?

No single TREC book — each course has its own standard textbook (Dearborn / Kaplan):

Principles I and II: Modern Real Estate Practice in Texas, 19th edition (DeHay) — print ISBN 978-1-0788-4192-4; eBook ISBN 978-1-0788-4193-1

Law of Agency: Texas Real Estate Agency, 9th edition

Law of Contracts: Texas Law of Contracts, 4th edition

Promulgated Contract Forms: Texas Promulgated Forms, 5th edition, with supplement

Real Estate Finance: Essentials of Real Estate Finance, 16th edition

Online packages usually build the reading into the course or bundle eBooks by tier; classroom and college programs sell the books separately

Free from Pearson VUE: the candidate handbook and the exam content outlines
